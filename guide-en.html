1. About this guide

This guide helps doctors, midwives, nurses and medical recorders choose the right ICD-10 code for every patient. Correct codes give accurate hospital statistics, HIS reports, disease surveillance and quality indicators (CHAS), and they help the next clinician understand the patient’s problems.

Every code in this guide comes from the KCPH master list ICD10-Diagnosis_Updated_9. Tap a code (for example J18.9) to open it in the ICD-10 Finder.

This guide supports, and does not replace, Ministry of Health and HIS instructions. Where the national rules differ, follow the national rules.

2. ICD-10 at KCPH

  • The KCPH master list has 96,693 codes. It follows the detailed ICD-10-CM structure, which has more digits than the basic WHO ICD-10.
  • 687 codes have a Khmer name. These are marked ★ in the Finder and are the codes KCPH uses most.
  • KCPH also has local codes that are not part of standard ICD-10, for example Z36.4.0–Z36.4.3 (ANC visits 1–4), Z39.0.0 (PNC visit 1), I10.0, I10.9, and local meanings for A91–A91.3 (dengue). Use them as your HIS requires. Other facilities should check with their HIS before using them.

3. How to read a code

Every code starts with a letter and two digits (the category). Each extra character adds detail.

Codable or heading? A code that has more specific codes under it is a heading and should not be recorded. For example J18 is a heading; record J18.9. The Finder marks codes as ✓ Codable or Heading.

4. Ten rules for correct coding

1
Code only what the clinician documented. Coders do not diagnose. If the record is unclear, ask the doctor.
2
Main condition first. The first code is the condition mainly responsible for the visit or admission (after investigation). Then add complications and other conditions that were treated or affected care.
3
Use the most specific codable code. Never record a heading. Choose the code that matches the documented type, site, side, severity, trimester and encounter.
4
Avoid “unspecified” when details are known. If the malaria test shows P. falciparum, use B50.9, not B54.
5
Replace symptom codes with the confirmed diagnosis. Use R codes (for example R50.9 fever) only when no diagnosis has been made.
6
Use combination codes. When one code describes two conditions together, use it instead of two separate codes, for example I11.0 hypertensive heart disease with heart failure, or E11.22 type 2 diabetes with diabetic chronic kidney disease.
7
Pregnant women: O codes come first. A condition in pregnancy, childbirth or the puerperium is coded with the O chapter unless the pregnancy is truly incidental (Z33.1).
8
Injury and poisoning: add the cause. Add an external cause code (V–Y) after the injury or poisoning code. External cause codes are never the main diagnosis.
9
Severity and stage matter. Record the depression severity, CKD stage, sepsis with or without shock, asthma exacerbation, and so on. These details change the risk colour and the treatment.
10
One code once. Do not repeat the same code, and do not code conditions that no longer affect care.

5. Choosing and ordering diagnoses

  • Main diagnosis (inpatient): the condition established after study as chiefly responsible for the admission.
  • Main diagnosis (OPD/ER): the main reason for the visit.
  • Other diagnoses: complications and coexisting conditions that were treated, investigated or extended the stay. Do not code old problems that no longer matter.
  • Uncertain diagnoses: for inpatients, a diagnosis written at discharge as “probable” or “suspected” is coded as if confirmed. For OPD visits, code the symptoms or signs instead. Follow your HIS rule if it is different.
  • Two equal conditions: put first the one that used more resources or was the reason for admission.
Example: admitted with pneumonia, developed acute respiratory failure, known COPD: J18.9 → J96.01 → J44.9.

6. Pregnancy, delivery and the puerperium (O, Z3x)

  • Normal ANC visit: Z34.0x (first pregnancy) or Z34.8x (later pregnancy), with the trimester digit, for example Z34.83. KCPH also records the visit number with Z36.4.0–Z36.4.3 (ANC 1–4).
  • High-risk pregnancy follow-up: O09.x (supervision of high-risk pregnancy).
  • Complications: use the O code with the trimester, for example O14.13 severe pre-eclampsia, third trimester, or O99.013 anaemia in pregnancy, third trimester (add D50.9 to show the type of anaemia).
  • Trimesters are counted from the first day of the last menstrual period: first trimester under 14 weeks, second 14 to under 28 weeks, third 28 weeks onward.
  • Delivery: code the delivery (for example O80, O82) and its complications, plus the outcome of delivery on the mother’s record, for example Z37.0 single live birth.
  • Postpartum: complications use O codes with “puerperium”, for example O72.1 immediate postpartum haemorrhage. KCPH PNC visits: Z39.0.0–Z39.0.4.

7. Newborns (P, Z38)

  • The baby’s birth record starts with Z38.0x, for example Z38.00 single liveborn, born in hospital, delivered vaginally.
  • Then add newborn conditions from the P chapter, for example P59.9 neonatal jaundice, P07.15 low birth weight 1250–1499 g, P22.0 respiratory distress syndrome.
  • Never use P codes on the mother’s record, or O codes on the baby’s record.

8. Injuries and external causes (S, T, V–Y)

  1. Injury code with the side and the 7th character: S52.502A.
  2. External cause, meaning how it happened: V29.9XXA motorcycle rider in a traffic accident, or W19.XXXA fall.
  3. Optional place: Y92.410 street or highway.
Use A while the patient receives active treatment (ER, surgery, admission). Use D for later visits during healing, such as dressing changes or cast checks. Use S for long-term complications of an old injury.

9. Poisoning and self-harm (T36–T65)

  • Choose the substance code. The 6th character gives the intent: 1 = accidental, 2 = intentional self-harm, 3 = assault, 4 = undetermined. The 7th character gives the encounter.
  • Code the effects as well, for example coma or respiratory failure.
  • Self-harm at KCPH: add T14.91 suicide attempt and the cause code X60, and refer the patient for mental health review. On later visits, record the history with Z91.5.
  • Snake bite: T63.0x. Anaphylaxis: T78.2.

10. Infections and sepsis

  • Sepsis: code the sepsis first (A41.9, or the organism, for example A41.51 E. coli), then the source (for example N39.0), then R65.20 severe sepsis or R65.21 septic shock.
  • HIV: the KCPH list has B20 only, so add the opportunistic infection (for example A15.0 lung TB). HIV positive without symptoms: Z21.
  • Dengue (KCPH local meanings): A90 dengue fever, A91 without shock, A91.1 with shock, A91.2 suspected, A91.3 probable.
  • Malaria: use the species code after the test (B50.9 falciparum, B51.9 vivax).
  • Notifiable diseases (TB, cholera, dengue, malaria, rabies, tetanus, measles and others) must also be reported to the surveillance focal point.

11. Chronic diseases (NCD)

  • Hypertension: in the KCPH list I10 has the local codes I10.0 and I10.9; use the one your HIS requires. With heart disease use I11.x, with chronic kidney disease I12.x, with both I13.x.
  • Type 2 diabetes: E11.9 without complications. With complications use the combination code, for example E11.22 with diabetic CKD plus the stage N18.3x, or E11.65 with hyperglycaemia. Gestational diabetes is an O code (O24.41x).
  • Stroke: I64 only until CT is done, then I63.9 (infarct) or I61.9 (bleed). On follow-up visits code the after-effects with I69.3x, not a new acute stroke.
  • COPD and asthma: show an exacerbation (J44.1, J45.901) or status asthmaticus (J45.902).

12. Mental health, cancer, symptoms and Z codes

Mental health

  • Depression: always give the severity (F32.1 moderate). Avoid F32.9 when the severity is known.
  • Alcohol and drugs: give the substance and the pattern (harmful use, dependence F10.20, withdrawal with delirium F10.231).
  • Always ask about suicide and record R45.851 when suicidal thoughts are present.

Cancer (neoplasms)

  • C codes = malignant, D00–D09 = in situ, D10–D36 = benign, D37–D49 = uncertain behaviour. Give the site, for example C53.9 cervix uteri, or D25.9 uterine leiomyoma (fibroid).

Symptoms (R) and factors (Z)

  • Use R codes only when no diagnosis is made. Use Z codes for visits that are not for a disease, for example vaccination (Z23), contraception (Z30.0x), check-ups (Z00.00) or follow-up after treatment (Z09).

15. What doctors should write

A coder can only code what is written. Please document:

  • Type and cause: organism (E. coli, P. falciparum), substance, mechanism of injury
  • Site and side: left or right, upper or lower, exact bone or organ
  • Severity and stage: mild, moderate or severe; CKD stage; with or without shock; with psychotic features
  • Acute or chronic, first episode or recurrent, exacerbation
  • Pregnancy: gestational age in weeks, gravida and parity, ANC visit number
  • Links between conditions: “due to”, “secondary to”, “complicated by”
  • Final diagnosis at discharge, replacing working diagnoses and symptoms

16. KCPH risk colours

For five services (Maternal & Newborn, NCD, Internal Medicine, Surgery, Mental Health), each code carries a KCPH risk colour:

ColourMeaningAction
RED · HighLife-threatening or danger signAct now; senior doctor; emergency protocol; consider ICU or referral
YELLOW · ModerateNeeds a doctor’s assessmentWritten plan the same day; often admit or follow up closely
GREEN · LowRoutine or mild conditionUsual care and scheduled follow-up
Colours are a triage aid prepared at KCPH. They do not replace clinical judgement, triage scores or national protocols.

17. Checklist before you sign or enter the record

  • The main diagnosis is first
  • Every code is ✓ Codable (not a heading)
  • Side, trimester, stage, severity and 7th character are filled in
  • Symptom codes are replaced by confirmed diagnoses
  • Pregnant or postpartum women have O codes; newborns have Z38/P codes
  • Injuries and poisonings have the external cause (and intent)
  • Sepsis has its source and its severity
  • No duplicate codes; KCPH local codes are used as HIS requires
  • Notifiable diseases are reported

18. Using the KCPH ICD-10 Finder

  1. Search by code (J18, J189), English words, Khmer words (រលាកសួត, ទឹកនោមផ្អែម) or abbreviations (HTN, COPD, PPH). Spelling mistakes are corrected automatically, and on Chrome or Android you can tap the 🎤 button to search by voice.
  2. Set your department (for example Maternal & Newborn) so your codes come first.
  3. On each result, tap ⧉ to copy the code for the EMR or ＋ to add it to the patient list.
  4. Tap a code to see whether it is codable, its KCPH risk colour, coding notes and more specific codes.
  5. My list: put the main diagnosis first, then copy, print or download to Excel.
  6. Check codes: paste codes from the EMR or a report to find heading codes, unknown codes and duplicates.
  7. The Finder works offline after the first visit, and you can install it on your phone (Help › Install).

19. Smart tools and free AI (version 1.7)

  1. Write like a doctor. The search understands shorthand and context: k/c HTN, T2DM + CKD 3, G2P1 34 wks anaemia, lt # femur, h/o TB, lay words (heart attack, loose motion) and Khmer (មានផ្ទៃពោះ ៧ ខែ ក្រឡាភ្លើង). Weeks of pregnancy choose the trimester; "PE" means pre-eclampsia in pregnancy and pulmonary embolism otherwise.
  2. Several diagnoses at once. Separate them with commas: the Finder shows the best 3 codes for each, coding tips (combination codes, external causes, sepsis order…) and ＋ Add all best codes.
  3. Free AI. When you are online and type a sentence, the AI (Google Gemini) also suggests codes with a short reason. Only codes that exist in the KCPH list are shown. You can turn this off in Help › Settings.
  4. 📷 Photo / text → codes. Take a photo of the diagnoses (handwriting works) or paste a discharge summary. The AI lists every diagnosis in order (main, other, external cause). Tick the codes to keep and add them to My list.
  5. 🧠 Check & audit codes. The audit scores a code list (0–100) and explains problems: wrong order, missing combination codes (E11.22, I11.0, I12.9, I13.0), missing external cause or Z37, mother and baby codes mixed, symptom codes that are not needed. AI audit gives a second opinion.
  6. 💾 Disease register. In My list, enter date, sex, age and OPD/IPD, then Save & start next patient. Only the date, sex, age group, codes and department are saved — never the name or patient number.
  7. 📈 Disease statistics. Monthly top 10 diagnoses, age group × sex, ICD-10 chapters and departments for this device or your whole facility, with Excel and PDF — ready for HIS reports and quality meetings.
  8. 🎓 Training & quiz. 32 real cases (maternal & newborn, NCD, surgery, mental health, children) with scores and explanations. Results appear in the monthly report for training follow-up.

Privacy: the free AI service may be used by Google to improve its products. Never type or photograph a patient’s name, ID number, phone or address — cover or crop them first. Always check AI codes against the record: the final decision is the clinician’s.
